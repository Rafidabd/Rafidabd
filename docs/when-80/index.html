When 80 Doesn't
Mean the Same

Can a lower mark still mean a student performed better relative to their peers?

I'm studying how raw examination marks compare with students' relative standing across the recurring assessments at NDC. I started asking this after my own academic analytics system made a prediction that went wrong.

Read the study briefSee the notes on GitHub
THE QUESTION, IN NUMBERSILLUSTRATION ONLY

Two quizzes. A different interpretation.

Suppose a student gets 90 in one quiz and 80 in another, while the second quiz also has lower scores among the participating students.

RAW MARKS90 → 80

The student's mark falls.

SAMPLE-RELATIVE Z-SCORES+1.0 → +1.5

Their relative position rises.

Not research results. Made-up example: means of 85 and 65; standard deviations of 5 and 10. This does not prove either examination was harder.

01 / Where it started

A result I couldn't explain with my model.

While building the Bangladesh Academic Analytics System, I used past marks to estimate my next mathematics quiz result. The program predicted at least 80. I got 55. That was the highest mark in my group.

The score made me question what the software was missing. Exams differ in questions, marking and other conditions. So what does a rise or fall in raw marks really tell us?

When a student's marks change, how often does their standing among others taking the same assessment move in another direction?
02 / Planned method

Follow the same students across exams.

I'm collecting Term, Sent-Up 1 and Sent-Up 2 report cards from the NDC HSC 2026 cohort. They include individual quiz marks and major-exam results. The report cards' weighted totals will be treated separately from the component exams.

01

Read the records

Check the reports and record subjects, papers, quizzes, examination scores and missing values without guessing.

02

Compare within exams

For suitable subject and assessment groups, compare raw marks with positions in the participating sample's score distribution.

03

Test disagreements

Count where raw and relative changes point in opposite directions, then check how sensitive that result is to the method.

Important limit: relative standing in a volunteer sample isn't an official NDC rank or a direct measure of exam difficulty. The aim is to test descriptions, not prove a student's true ability.
