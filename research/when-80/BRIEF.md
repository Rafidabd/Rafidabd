# When 80 Doesn't Mean the Same

*A longitudinal study of academic performance across examinations at Notre Dame College, Dhaka*

**Independent research · Work-in-progress summary · October 31, 2026**

## Abstract

An early version of my academic analytics system predicted that I would score at least 80 on a mathematics quiz. I scored 55—the highest mark in my group. The prediction made me wonder what I'd left out when comparing results from one exam to another.

This ongoing study examines that question using historical academic records from Notre Dame College's HSC 2026 cohort. Students' Term, Sent-Up 1 and Sent-Up 2 report cards contain marks from recurring quizzes and major examinations. The planned analysis will reconstruct subject-level histories and compare changes in raw percentage scores with changes in relative standing among students with marks available for both assessments in a comparison.

The main question is how often these two descriptions of academic progress disagree. Comparisons will be made within appropriate subjects and assessment types using a matched set of participants for each pair of assessments, with checks for incomplete records and small comparison groups. I'll also consider what these disagreements mean for the longitudinal analyses in my Python/Flask-based Bangladesh Academic Analytics System.

Data collection is underway. **No statistical findings or claims of better predictive accuracy are reported yet.** Relative standing within a volunteer sample is not a direct measure of examination difficulty or underlying student ability. The study is limited to participating students at one institution.

**Research question:** When a student's marks rise or fall between examinations, does their standing among students taking those same examinations always move in the same direction?

**Current stage:** Data collection; extraction, verification and analysis pending.
