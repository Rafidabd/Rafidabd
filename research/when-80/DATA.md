# About the data

For this study, I've been collecting academic records from Notre Dame College's HSC 2026 batch. As of November 14, 2026, I have **31 observed student histories**: 20 transcribed from supplied report cards and 11 originally entered from handwritten notes, which I have since checked against their source records. Some report-card fields remain incomplete or missing; checking a history against its source does not mean every quiz mark is available. I also created four synthetic histories to test the software; those aren't student observations.

Each report card has more useful detail than its final average. The Term report shows three quiz marks and an exam mark for each subject. Sent Up I adds four quizzes and another exam, and Sent Up II includes five quiz columns and its exam. Not every subject has a mark in every quiz column, so a dash isn't automatically a zero. I've kept entries such as `Ex` and `Ab` as printed instead of treating them all the same.

The private working spreadsheet contains 35 entries in total: 31 observed student histories and four separately labeled synthetic software-test histories. **Those 35 entries do not represent 35 students**, and the entire 31-student set is not the sample for the published pilot. **The first public results use only the 20 histories transcribed from supplied report cards.** The other 11 observed histories have now been source-checked but were not included in those original calculations. Synthetic test histories are not used in research analyses, and I have not published the private student-level data.

Any expanded analysis will use only marks that I can tie back to collected records. I also won't upload the report card images or individual level data here; they include personal academic information.

What I'm interested in is fairly simple to explain: if someone's mark drops from one quiz to another, did their standing among the *same participating students* drop too? For now, that question is more useful than a claim about which paper was harder or which measure is fairer.
