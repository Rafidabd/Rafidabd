# Research notes

I'm studying results from Notre Dame College's HSC 2026 batch. What started as a question about one bad mathematics prediction has turned into a much bigger job of going through old report cards.

**November 14, 2026**

I have now checked the 11 histories originally entered from handwritten notes against their source records. This brings the observed student histories to 31: 20 transcribed from supplied report cards and 11 source-checked handwritten histories. Four additional synthetic histories remain separate software-test entries, not student observations. The published October 31 pilot still uses only the original 20 report-card histories, and I have not recalculated its figures for all 31.

**October 31, 2026**

My working file has 20 student histories transcribed from supplied report cards and 11 more entered from my handwritten notes. I also made four synthetic histories while testing the software. Those four aren't student observations, and the entries from handwritten notes still need checking against their sources. I've been putting the marks into a consistent format so I can compare the same subject and quiz round across students.

One thing I had to sort out early was how NDC calculates its report card totals. The Sent Up results include marks from earlier assessments, so they aren't three independent exams. I'm keeping the individual quiz and exam marks separate from those weighted totals.

I'm also being careful about what the comparisons can show. A lower class average might be related to a harder paper, but that isn't something I can prove from these records alone. And a student's standing among the people who shared their marks isn't the same as their official NDC position.

I've now run an initial descriptive comparison using the 20 histories I can trace to supplied report cards. Across 814 comparisons where both the raw score and standing relative to others in the sample changed, they moved in opposite directions 148 times (18.2%). I'm treating this as a pilot result, not a conclusion about the entire college. The [results note](RESULTS.md) explains the calculation and its limits.

The report cards and spreadsheet containing individual student marks are private. I have a separate working copy for software tests, but generated example records aren't research observations.
